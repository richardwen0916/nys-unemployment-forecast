# Release timing and feature policy

The unchanged metadata are the authority: [release rules](data/processed/release_rules.csv) and [observation calendar](data/processed/release_calendar.csv). Every verified row carries its official source URL. The panel's date is the economic reference month; `available_at` is a publication/public-by timestamp; the numerical vintage is a separate unresolved dimension.

For the four core variables, missing calendar entries use explicitly **estimated** fallbacks: NYUR and NY payroll reference month-end +45 calendar days, January +75 days; USUR month-end +14 days; NY weekly claims Saturday +14 days. Calendar-first verified dates and explicit irregular holdbacks override matching fallback observations. Unknown times on a date-only release become New York day-end. These are policies, not invented historical release dates; conservative lags are not guarantees against every undocumented exceptional delay.

The payroll audit verified all 48 reference months in 2016–2019 and April–December 2015, plus selected older, benchmark, COVID, shutdown and 2026 observations. Matching series is **seasonally adjusted total nonfarm employment**, not private payrolls or NSA counts. [BLS state release archive](https://www.bls.gov/bls/news-release/laus.htm) and [NYNA source](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NYNA) support the definition. November 2023 payroll was public in the [NYS DOL December 21 report, page 2](https://dol.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/12/nys-private-sector-employment-up-5500-jobs-in-november-2023.pdf) and [BLS December 22 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/laus_12222023.htm). January benchmark releases may be delayed into March; later exceptional dates stay observation-specific. The retained payroll tests check exact validation timestamps, benchmark delays and irregular releases.

The complete monthly NY claims average is available only after the month is complete and all expected weekly constituents have been published; the constituent mean must match the panel. Missing data are never manufactured. In strict `allow_estimated=False` mode all relevant constituents need verified timing, so claims may remain unavailable; baseline runs retain documented estimated policies.

Claims STL uses available positive raw monthly means, robust log-STL with period 12 and at least 36 months. Internal gaps raise instead of being interpolated. The full-sample `ny_claims_avg_sa` is always blocked. Future changes to raw claims cannot affect a historical feature window. Calendar differences are computed before selecting latest observed levels, preventing gaps from becoming fictitious one-month changes.

[ALFRED vintage documentation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/alfred.html) explains why publication time does not establish which historical numeric version was known. No numerical vintages are reconstructed here. The original dictionary's approximate release-lag descriptions are historical documentation; use the release CSVs and code for actual filtering.
